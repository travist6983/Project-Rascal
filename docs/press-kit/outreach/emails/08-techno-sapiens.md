**Subject:** A private place for the things you'd otherwise post

```text
Dr. Nesi,

In July you wrote that parents don't need more scare tactics about technology. Apps for remembering a childhood mostly sell on fear of forgetting. I built one that doesn't.

I'm a dad who forgets to take pictures. When I remember, a year later I can't say what was happening or why I took it. The camera roll remembers what my kid looked like, not what they were like. Pocket Chronicle is my fix: two prompts a day. One is a question ("What did they say this week that you want to remember exactly?"). The other is a mission: a photo, a video, their voice, or something to do together. Each is filed with the prompt, the date and their exact age, on a timeline they inherit.

No feed, no likes, no ad or analytics SDK. No streaks, no guilt copy. A missed day is not a failure, and the app never says it was.

The first 14 days are full access, no card, nothing to cancel. After that the daily question stays free forever; photos, video and voice are part of membership, $5.99 a month or $59.99 a year.
```
