MY PICK: A
No shadow. At 4% over cream the shadow is not a legible difference at rest — but the locked card sits directly under a live one that has it, and the only way a parent ever perceives a 4% shadow is as a difference between two adjacent cards. Dropping it is the quietest available signal and costs nothing.
AGAINST C
terracottaSoft at any opacity is the fill a live mission wears. Washing it makes the locked card look like a live mission rendering badly, which is the failure mode you named.
AGAINST D
The kind glyph is already on the card, in the tile, 34pt. A second mark 13pt below it says nothing the first one didn't, and the system's own rule hides a glyph that repeats the text beside it. It also reads as the ring of a disabled control.

THE ROW
No card, no fill, no hairline, no glyph. metadata 13 inkMuted left, action right, inset 4 from the card edge, sitting in the same 12 card gap. It is a caption on the day, not an item in it.
WHY THIS WINS
One row is identical on a weekday and a weekend, so the free board never grows a second and third reminder as the week ends. It cannot read as an error state, a disabled control or an ad, because it is not a control. And it survives AX3, which the card does not.
WHAT IT COSTS
The free parent stops learning what a mission is. 1a teaches "a picture of him, and the reason you took it" every morning; this teaches nothing. If that education is the point of the locked card, keep the card — but then the honest version is one card, not two on a weekend.
COPY ⚠
Both strings are verbatim (§1 footer, §3 notice action). Neither was written for this row. A one-line form belongs in the file — proposed, not drawn: Missions are included with membership.
CONTRAST ⚠
Action drawn in terracottaDeep (6.31:1). terracotta at 13pt measures 4.26:1 on canvas and fails AA. Same substitution everywhere terracotta carries a small string below.
